0. Overview: The Essence of Data Analysis0. Overview: The Essence of Data Analysis

> Many people think glancing at a report counts as data analysis. If you don't understand the transformation logic between "data, information, and insight," you'll be trapped in the overwhelming details of numbers. The purpose of this section is to help you build a big-picture view and understand that the ultimate goal of data analysis is not "reporting," but "decision-making."> Many people think glancing at a report counts as data analysis. If you don't understand the transformation logic between "data, information, and insight," you'll be trapped in the overwhelming details of numbers. The purpose of this section is to help you build a big-picture view and understand that the ultimate goal of data analysis is not "reporting," but "decision-making."

Data analysis is not simply "report summarization," but a process of dimensionality reduction and feature extraction.Data analysis is not simply "report summarization," but a process of dimensionality reduction and feature extraction.

  • Raw Data: Scattered, unordered records (e.g., User A clicked Button B at 10:01).Raw Data: Scattered, unordered records (e.g., User A clicked Button B at 10:01).
  • Information: Processed data (e.g., 30% of users clicked Button B today).Information: Processed data (e.g., 30% of users clicked Button B today).
  • Insight: Discovering patterns behind the data (e.g., Button B's click rate is much higher on mobile than desktop, indicating mobile users rely more on this feature).Insight: Discovering patterns behind the data (e.g., Button B's click rate is much higher on mobile than desktop, indicating mobile users rely more on this feature).

Our goal is to build a systematic analysis framework that drives business growth through a closed loop of "observe → decompose → pinpoint → decide."Our goal is to build a systematic analysis framework that drives business growth through a closed loop of "observe → decompose → pinpoint → decide."

1. Descriptive Statistics: Summarizing the Big Picture in One Sentence1. Descriptive Statistics: Summarizing the Big Picture in One Sentence

> When faced with 100,000 rows of data, you can't review each one individually. You need the ability to "compress information," using a minimal set of metrics to precisely capture the pulse of the data. If you don't understand the statistical traps of mean and median, you'll be misled by extreme values when analyzing business performance (such as average spending per user), leading to absurd conclusions.> When faced with 100,000 rows of data, you can't review each one individually. You need the ability to "compress information," using a minimal set of metrics to precisely capture the pulse of the data. If you don't understand the statistical traps of mean and median, you'll be misled by extreme values when analyzing business performance (such as average spending per user), leading to absurd conclusions.

When a dataset has tens of thousands of records, we need to use a small number of "representative metrics" to describe its overall profile.When a dataset has tens of thousands of records, we need to use a small number of "representative metrics" to describe its overall profile.

1.1 Mean: The Baseline of Overall Level1.1 Mean: The Baseline of Overall Level

The mean (arithmetic average) is the most intuitive metric.The mean (arithmetic average) is the most intuitive metric.

  • Calculation logic: The sum of all values divided by the total count of data.Calculation logic: The sum of all values divided by the total count of data.
  • Limitation: It is highly susceptible to interference from extreme outliers.Limitation: It is highly susceptible to interference from extreme outliers.
  • Example: If 9 employees earn 5k/month and the boss earns 100k/month, the average salary appears to be 14.5k. At this point, the mean does not truly represent the income level of most employees.Example: If 9 employees earn 5k/month and the boss earns 100k/month, the average salary appears to be 14.5k. At this point, the mean does not truly represent the income level of most employees.

1.2 Median and Mode1.2 Median and Mode

  • Median: Sort the data from smallest to largest and take the middle value. It effectively resists interference from outliers and truly reflects the typical "middle layer" level.Median: Sort the data from smallest to largest and take the middle value. It effectively resists interference from outliers and truly reflects the typical "middle layer" level.
  • Mode: The value that appears most frequently in the dataset. When analyzing "users' favorite products" or "most common error codes," the mode most directly indicates group tendencies.Mode: The value that appears most frequently in the dataset. When analyzing "users' favorite products" or "most common error codes," the mode most directly indicates group tendencies.

1.3 Standard Deviation: The "Width" of Distribution1.3 Standard Deviation: The "Width" of Distribution

It describes the magnitude of fluctuation of data points around the mean.It describes the magnitude of fluctuation of data points around the mean.

  • Low standard deviation: Data is very concentrated, and the mean is highly representative (e.g., component dimensions on a factory assembly line).Low standard deviation: Data is very concentrated, and the mean is highly representative (e.g., component dimensions on a factory assembly line).
  • High standard deviation: Data is widely scattered, with significant individual differences.High standard deviation: Data is widely scattered, with significant individual differences.
  • Significance: In performance monitoring, high standard deviation often indicates insufficient system stability, with many extremely slow "long-tail requests."Significance: In performance monitoring, high standard deviation often indicates insufficient system stability, with many extremely slow "long-tail requests."

2. Data Aggregation: Mining Micro-Patterns in Groups2. Data Aggregation: Mining Micro-Patterns in Groups

> "Average conversion rate for all users is 5%" is often a meaningless truth. You must learn how to "slice" the data to discover the huge differences between different regions, channels, and device types. Aggregation analysis lets you pierce through the "one-size-fits-all" averages and directly reach the real business pain points that were hidden.> "Average conversion rate for all users is 5%" is often a meaningless truth. You must learn how to "slice" the data to discover the huge differences between different regions, channels, and device types. Aggregation analysis lets you pierce through the "one-size-fits-all" averages and directly reach the real business pain points that were hidden.

Individual behavior is often random, but group behavior follows statistical patterns. The core of Data Aggregation lies in "slicing" populations along specific dimensions.Individual behavior is often random, but group behavior follows statistical patterns. The core of Data Aggregation lies in "slicing" populations along specific dimensions.

2.1 The Core Logic of Aggregation: Split-Apply-Combine2.1 The Core Logic of Aggregation: Split-Apply-Combine

  1. Split: Group by an attribute (e.g., city, registration channel, new vs. returning users).Split: Group by an attribute (e.g., city, registration channel, new vs. returning users).
  2. Apply: Execute aggregation functions within each group, such as COUNT(), SUM(), AVG().Apply: Execute aggregation functions within each group, such as COUNT(), SUM(), AVG().
  3. Combine: Compare results across different groups to identify differences.Combine: Compare results across different groups to identify differences.

    4. 2.2 Motivation for Groupinging (Group By) Essential2.2 Motivation for Groupinging (Group By) Essential

    Aggregated data often hides problems. For example, overall conversion rates may be rising, but after splitting, you find that a surge in "Shanghai" is pulling up the overall average while all other regions are declining. Through aggregation analysis, we can precisely pinpoint the best or worst-performing segments from the "blended average."Aggregated data often hides problems. For example, overall conversion rates may be rising, but after splitting, you find that a surge in "Shanghai" is pulling up the overall average while all other regions are declining. Through aggregation analysis, we can precisely pinpoint the best or worst-performing segments from the "blended average."

Learning this section transforms "business optimization" from blind guessing into targeted development, directing resources to the points with the highest conversion returns.> You invested significant resources to acquire users, but conversions are minimal — was all the money wasted? The funnel model tells you exactly where users stumbled.     3.1 Core Conversion Metrics3.1 Core Conversion Metrics

    • Overall conversion rate: Total users who completed the end / Total users who entered the start.Overall conversion rate: Total users who completed the end / Total users who entered the start.
    • Step conversion rate: Users at current step / Users at previous step (reflects the pass-through efficiency of that step).Step conversion rate: Users at current step / Users at previous step (reflects the pass-through efficiency of that step).
    • Drop-off rate: 1 - Step conversion rate.Drop-off rate: 1 - Step conversion rate.

    3.2 In-Depth Analysis Approach3.2 In-Depth Analysis Approach

    If the drop-off rate at a particular step is abnormally high, it indicates experience friction at that point. For example:If the drop-off rate at a particular step is abnormally high, it indicates experience friction at that point. For example:

    • High drop-off on the registration page: The form is too complex or verification codes aren't being received.High drop-off on the registration page: The form is too complex or verification codes aren't being received.
    • Drop-off at payment method selection: Too few payment options or slow redirect loading.Drop-off at payment method selection: Too few payment options or slow redirect loading.

    Investing effort in optimizing the narrowest part of the funnel typically yields the greatest returns.Investing effort in optimizing the narrowest part of the funnel typically yields the greatest returns.

    4. Retention Analysis: The "Hardcore" Health Check for Your Product4. Retention Analysis: The "Hardcore" Health Check for Your Product

    > Retention is the primary gold standard for product value. If user acquisition is filling a bucket with water, retention is checking whether the bucket leaks. If you only know how to look at total visits (traffic) but can't analyze retention (keeping users), you can't determine whether the product is growing healthily or playing a numbers game destined to collapse.> Retention is the primary gold standard for product value. If user acquisition is filling a bucket with water, retention is checking whether the bucket leaks. If you only know how to look at total visits (traffic) but can't analyze retention (keeping users), you can't determine whether the product is growing healthily or playing a numbers game destined to collapse.

    User growth doesn't equal success — retaining users is the core value. Retention rate measures the proportion of users who return after a specific time period.User growth doesn't equal success — retaining users is the core value. Retention rate measures the proportion of users who return after a specific time period.

    4.1 Core Time Windows4.1 Core Time Windows

    • Day 1 Retention: Focuses on "first impression." Did users perceive the core value within 24 hours of their first visit?Day 1 Retention: Focuses on "first impression." Did users perceive the core value within 24 hours of their first visit?
    • Day 7 Retention: Focuses on "habit formation." Did users develop a pattern of periodic usage within the first week?Day 7 Retention: Focuses on "habit formation." Did users develop a pattern of periodic usage within the first week?
    • Day 30 Retention: Focuses on "long-term stickiness." It determines the product's survival ceiling.Day 30 Retention: Focuses on "long-term stickiness." It determines the product's survival ceiling.

    4.2 Retention Curve Shapes: Determining PMF4.2 Retention Curve Shapes: Determining PMF

    • Continuous decline to zero: The product doesn't solve user pain points, or it's acquiring the wrong user segments.Continuous decline to zero: The product doesn't solve user pain points, or it's acquiring the wrong user segments.
    • Stabilization (long tail): The product has achieved PMF (Product-Market Fit), possessing a group of loyal, sticky users and a foundation for scaled growth.Stabilization (long tail): The product has achieved PMF (Product-Market Fit), possessing a group of loyal, sticky users and a foundation for scaled growth.
